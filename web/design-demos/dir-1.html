The method

A fixed order.
Room for human judgment.

Each quotation follows the same five steps. The aim is to make uncertainty visible and ask a person to check it, with evidence they can inspect.

  1. 1

    Read the PDF

    Start with the supplier’s own words.

  2. 2

    Extract facts

    Record prices, terms and the supporting text.

  3. 3

    Check the rules

    Compare with the request and company policy.

  4. 4

    Make a decision

    Approve, reject or flag for a person.

  5. 5

    Show the evidence

    Give reasons and exact source excerpts.

Honest limits

What these results
can’t tell us.

A useful demonstration should make its boundaries as easy to see as its best result.

01

Development data, not unseen tests

All ten PDFs were visible while building. These scores do not measure accuracy on unseen documents.

02

The final approach got a policy change

After viewing packs 06 and 08, policy changed to read the rules literally. Approaches 1–3 did not get that change: 9 versus 7 is not like for like.

03

Single runs; no independent human audit

Model output can vary. An AI assistant audited the fact labels, without independent human reviewers.

04

Approvals have no code guard

Approach 4 relies on the judge, fail-closed handling and a prompt-injection rule. Injection testing covered only two packs and one model.

05

Hosted models receive document text

Hosted inference sends text to a third party. Every supplier document in this demonstration is fictional.

06

Fully local inference was too slow

The reported local experiment exceeded ten minutes per call on a 12 GB GPU. This is separate from the five-PDF rerun.
